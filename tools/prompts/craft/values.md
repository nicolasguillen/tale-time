# Values and themes

version: 1

What a story is *about*, and how to show it without saying it. Used by the seeds, bible, outline, text and editor steps. The bible names the story's value in `craft.value`.

Adapted from the kids-book-writer skill ([jamesrochabrun/skills](https://github.com/jamesrochabrun/skills/tree/main/skills/kids-book-writer), MIT) and reworked for branching stories that are read aloud. The rhyming, phonics and song parts were left out on purpose.

## Show, don't preach

The child learns the value by watching the hero live it and by choosing it at a fork. Nobody states the lesson.

- **Told (never):** "And so Mia learned that helping is good. We should always help our friends."
- **Shown:** "Mia looked at her own lunch: a sandwich, an apple, two cookies. She had so much. She took a deep breath and walked over. 'Want one of my cookies?'"

The last line of an ending is a feeling or a picture, never a moral.

## One value, and the child picks how

- **One main value per story**, plus up to two that come along (`craft.alsoValues`). One main problem, one main value.
- **The trunk carries the value.** The trunk is the pages every path shares. That's where you put the problem that calls for the value, the low point, and the moment it matters.
- **The forks choose *how* the hero lives the value, not *whether*.** Both options are good in different ways: brave or careful, help now or fetch help, ask or try, together or alone. That's how "no option is obviously wrong" and "values without preaching" fit together.
- **Never offer the unkind, dishonest or careless option.** If someone needs help, both options help them. If the hero is tempted to fib, both options tell the truth, now or after fixing things. A temptation can show up on a trunk page, but the child never has to choose it.
- **Consequences come naturally,** within a page or two: a good feeling, a new friend, a laugh, pride. Never a prize. ("Billy shared his toy, so he got a new bike" is wrong.)
- **The best ending shows the value at its fullest.** It takes two choices after the last merge (outline.md). The other endings still show the value, in a smaller, funnier or cozier way.

## The values

These are the ids `craft.value` takes. For each one: what it shows, its shape on the trunk, fork pairs that fit it, situations to use, and what to watch for.

### `kindness`: kindness and empathy
- **Shows:** noticing how someone else feels, and doing something about it.
- **Shape:** the hero notices someone sad, stuck or alone → helping costs them something small (time, a treat, their shyness) → they help → both feel better.
- **Forks:** help now, or fetch a friend or grown-up who can; cheer them up with a game, or with a song; share your snack, or share your turn.
- **Situations:** someone playing alone, a friend who's sad, a neighbor who needs a hand, someone who's different.
- **Watch:** the one being helped has feelings and a voice too. They aren't just a problem to solve.

### `courage`: courage and bravery
- **Shows:** trying even though you're afraid. It can be physical (a new slide), social (saying hello, speaking up), emotional (trying again after failing) or moral (standing up for someone).
- **Shape:** something new or a bit scary → the fear is real and named, and nobody laughs at it → support arrives, or the hero finds strength inside → they try anyway → it works, or they're proud they tried.
- **Forks:** the big one, or start with the small one; go first, or go with a friend; say it out loud, or show it.
- **Situations:** a first day, saying hello, performing, speaking up when something's wrong, trying again.
- **Watch:** physical courage never means a risk a child could copy (heights, water, fire, strangers). Keep courage small and real.

### `honesty`: honesty and integrity
- **Shows:** telling the truth when it's hard, owning up to a mistake, keeping a promise.
- **Shape:** the hero makes a small mistake on the trunk → they're tempted to hide it → they tell the truth → relief and trust, and they fix it together.
- **Forks:** both are honest. Tell right away, or fix it first and then tell; say it in words, or show what happened.
- **Situations:** something broken, the last cookie, a promise that's become a nuisance, admitting you don't know.
- **Watch:** the grown-up stays warm ("Thank you for telling me"). The mistake is small and can be fixed. The lie is never a fork option.

### `perseverance`: perseverance and growth mindset
- **Shows:** trying again, getting better with practice, mistakes that teach something.
- **Shape:** the hero wants to do something → the first try fails, in a funny way → they're frustrated and want to quit → encouragement or a new idea → a different approach → progress, not perfection → pride.
- **Forks:** try again the same way, or a new way; practice alone, or with a friend; take a break, or keep going.
- **Situations:** learning a skill, building or making something, reaching a small goal.
- **Watch:** failure is funny and safe, never humiliating. Each try looks different, which helps the pictures. Merge pages can't count tries made on branches, because stories are stateless: write "that didn't work either", without naming the try.

### `self-acceptance`: self-acceptance and confidence
- **Shows:** being yourself is enough, and a difference can be a strength.
- **Shape:** the hero feels different or not good enough → tries to hide it or change → a moment comes where their difference is exactly what's needed → pride.
- **Forks:** two ways to use the difference (light the way, or sing the signal); show it to one friend, or to everyone.
- **Watch:** the ones who laughed come round, and nobody is punished. Make the difference an invented quirk (pink polka dots, a giant laugh, tiny wings). Real differences (skin, bodies, disabilities, families) appear as normal, never as the problem.

### `friendship`: friendship and loyalty
- **Shows:** being a good friend, making up, forgiving, standing by a friend.
- **Shape:** two friends, or a new one → a disagreement or a test → the hero chooses the friendship over getting their own way → they're closer than before.
- **Forks:** play your game, or your friend's game; say sorry with words, or with something you made; make a new friend, or bring the old one along too.
- **Watch:** nobody gets dropped for someone new. More friends is better.

### `sharing`: sharing and generosity
- **Shows:** the joy of giving.
- **Shape:** the hero has something they treasure → someone else could use it → they want to keep it, and that feeling is normal → they share or give → a joy they didn't expect (a feeling, not a reward).
- **Forks:** share half, or take turns; give it, or share it.
- **Watch:** sharing never means giving everything away until nothing is left. The hero's feelings about their treasure are respected.

### `gratitude`: gratitude and appreciation
- **Shows:** noticing the good things you have, and saying thank you.
- **Shape:** the hero wants more or different → overlooks what's already there → a moment shows them what they have → they notice → they thank someone.
- **Forks:** thank them with a song, or with a drawing; tell them now, or make a surprise.
- **Watch:** what might be lost stays small and comes back (a rainy day, a lost mitten). Never a person or a pet.

### `inclusion`: respect and inclusion
- **Shows:** respecting differences, and including everyone.
- **Shape:** someone different arrives → a small misunderstanding, or someone left out → the hero learns about them → they find what they share and enjoy what's different → friends.
- **Forks:** invite them into your game, or join theirs; show them around, or ask them to show you something.
- **Watch:** the newcomer acts and speaks for themselves. A culture is shown specifically and with respect, never as a costume.

### `responsibility`: responsibility and helping
- **Shows:** looking after things and creatures, following through, helping out.
- **Shape:** the hero is given a job, or asks for one → forgets it or rushes it, on the trunk → a mild, natural consequence → they step up → pride.
- **Forks:** two orders or ways of doing the job; do it alone, or with a sibling.
- **Watch:** consequences are mild and can be fixed (a hungry puppy, not a sick one). The job is never skipped in a fork.

### `curiosity`: curiosity and wonder
- **Shows:** wondering, looking closer, asking questions.
- **Shape:** something puzzling → the hero investigates → discoveries → they understand, and find a new wonder.
- **Forks:** look closer, or ask someone who knows; follow the sound, or follow the tracks.
- **Watch:** curiosity never means touching wild animals, eating unknown things or wandering off (the bible's "never" list). Exploring stays within reach of a grown-up.

### `patience`: patience
- **Shows:** waiting, going slowly, letting things grow.
- **Shape:** the hero wants it now → rushing doesn't work, in a funny way → they wait and watch → it happens, and it's better for the wait.
- **Forks:** wait and watch, or help it along gently; count to ten, or sing while you wait.

### `asking-for-help`: asking for help
- **Shows:** that asking for help is a strength.
- **Shape:** the hero tries alone → gets stuck → asks → it works, together.
- **Forks:** ask a friend, or try once more yourself. Both are fine, and the best ending takes the asking.
- **Watch:** the helper is a known friend or grown-up, never a stranger.

### `caring-for-nature`: caring for nature
- **Shows:** looking after plants, animals and places.
- **Shape:** something in nature needs care → the hero notices → they care for it, with a grown-up for anything tricky → it thrives.
- **Forks:** plant it here, or there; give it water, or give it shade.
- **Watch:** caring means watching, giving space, planting and tidying up. Never touching or catching wild animals.

The last four (`curiosity`, `patience`, `asking-for-help`, `caring-for-nature`) are this project's additions. The skill lists only the last of them, as a theme with no detail.

## Which values for which band

| Band | Values that fit best | How deep it goes |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5 | sharing, kindness, friendship, small courage, patience, curiosity, gratitude, caring-for-nature | Concrete, immediate, positive, simple cause and effect. The value is one clear act. Basic feelings, no inner conflict. |
| 4–7 | all of them | Mild conflict, resolved within a page or two. Simple moral choices. The hero grows a little. |
| 7–10 | all of them, especially honesty, inclusion, perseverance, self-acceptance, friendship and responsibility | Inner conflict, two points of view, small dilemmas where both sides have a point, a longer arc. Still kind, still safe. |

A story can carry a second value or two (`alsoValues`). Self-acceptance often brings respect and kindness with it.

## Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Preachy:** "Everyone should share. Always share." | Let the hero discover it, and let the child choose it. |
| **Unrealistic reward:** sharing earns a new bike. | The reward is a feeling, a friend, pride, or a small, lasting gesture (a star that winks at the window). |
| **A villain:** "the mean bully was sent away forever." | Grumpy or unkind characters soften, or turn out to have a reason. Everyone can grow, and nobody is punished. |
| **Adult problems:** divorce, death, illness. | Not in this project unless the user asks for that story (ages.md). Keep to the child's own world. |
| **No stakes:** "Everything was perfect. The end." | A real, small challenge the hero cares about. |

## Representation

- Across the catalog, show different family structures, cultures, abilities, body types and incomes, and gender diversity.
- **Make diversity normal.** It isn't the lesson, unless the story is about it.
- **A universal theme, a specific character.** A specific, authentic hero, such as a child who lives between two languages, makes a universal value land harder.
- No stereotypes. When a story is rooted in a specific culture, list it in `review.md` as a judgment call, so the user can find a reader from that culture.

## Values worksheet (for the bible)

1. **Main value:** `craft.value`.
2. **What the hero needs to learn:** the small flaw in the hero's `personality`, for a growth arc.
3. **Story problem:** in the `pitch`. How does the problem call for the value?
4. **Where the child chooses it:** the forks, with their `why` in the outline.
5. **Natural consequence:** the page or two after those forks.
6. **Emotional payoff:** `craft.payoff`. How the child feels on the last page.

> "The best children's books don't teach lessons. They offer experiences that help children discover lessons for themselves."
